\# DESIGN SYSTEM

\# Premium Interactive Renovation Website



\## 01 — DESIGN SYSTEM PHILOSOPHY



The design system exists to create a premium, editorial, architectural and highly interactive visual language.



The website should feel designed as a complete visual experience rather than assembled from independent UI components.



The design system must support:



\- Strong visual hierarchy

\- Editorial typography

\- Cinematic photography

\- Asymmetrical layouts

\- Layered compositions

\- Responsive layouts

\- Reactive scrolling

\- High-end motion

\- Smooth transitions

\- Clear usability

\- Strong conversion



The visual system should remain consistent across every page while allowing individual sections to have their own composition.



Consistency does NOT mean repetition.



\---



\# 02 — CORE VISUAL PRINCIPLES



The visual language is built around five principles:



01\. TYPOGRAPHY

02\. PHOTOGRAPHY

03\. SPACE

04\. CONTRAST

05\. MOTION



These five elements should work together.



Do not treat motion as something added after the design.



Motion is part of the design system.



\---



\# 03 — VISUAL CHARACTER



The website should feel:



\- Architectural

\- Editorial

\- Warm

\- Sophisticated

\- Cinematic

\- Minimal

\- Confident

\- Human

\- Contemporary



It should feel premium without looking like a luxury-fashion clone.



It should feel artistic without sacrificing usability.



It should feel technically impressive without looking like a technology demo.



\---



\# 04 — COLOR SYSTEM



Primary colors:



\### Deep Black

Used for:



\- Dark sections

\- Navigation states

\- Hero overlays

\- Process sections

\- Footer

\- Strong contrast areas



\### Warm Off-White

Used for:



\- Main backgrounds

\- Large editorial sections

\- Text surfaces

\- Content areas



\### Stone / Sand

Used for:



\- Secondary backgrounds

\- Section transitions

\- Cards where appropriate

\- Warm visual balance



\### Muted Earth Tones

Used primarily through photography and subtle accents.



\### Restrained Olive / Forest

May be used sparingly when appropriate to the architectural photography.



\### Accent

Use one restrained warm accent such as muted bronze, taupe or warm gold.



The accent must remain subtle.



Do not use bright gold.



Do not use neon.



Do not use large colorful gradients.



\---



\# 05 — COLOR BEHAVIOR



Color should sometimes transition according to scroll position.



Examples:



\- Light section → dark section

\- Dark section → warm off-white section

\- Image overlay gradually becoming stronger

\- Text changing from dark → light as it enters a dark photographic area



These transitions should feel continuous rather than like abrupt background swaps.



\---



\# 06 — TYPOGRAPHY SYSTEM



Use two primary font categories.



\### DISPLAY FONT



Elegant editorial serif.



Used for:



\- Hero headlines

\- Major statements

\- Project titles

\- Section statements

\- Large editorial moments



Characteristics:



\- High contrast

\- Elegant

\- Sophisticated

\- Large scale

\- Generous line-height



\### UI / BODY FONT



Modern restrained sans-serif.



Used for:



\- Navigation

\- Body copy

\- Buttons

\- Metadata

\- Labels

\- Project information

\- Forms



Characteristics:



\- Clean

\- Highly readable

\- Neutral

\- Modern



\---



\# 07 — TYPOGRAPHIC SCALE



Typography should use dramatic scale differences.



Example hierarchy:



DISPLAY

→ extremely large



H1

→ very large



H2

→ large



H3

→ medium



BODY

→ comfortable reading size



LABEL

→ small uppercase / metadata



Do not make every heading the same size.



Large typography is an important part of the visual identity.



\---



\# 08 — EDITORIAL TYPOGRAPHY



Large headlines should sometimes intentionally occupy unusual portions of the layout.



Examples:



\- Text crossing over imagery

\- Large text partially leaving the grid

\- Large statements aligned vertically

\- Text revealed through masks

\- Words entering independently

\- Typography pinned while imagery moves



Typography should feel composed rather than merely formatted.



\---



\# 09 — TEXT REVEALS



Text reveals should generally use:



\- Masked vertical reveals

\- Subtle upward movement

\- Opacity

\- Small scale changes

\- Character or word-level sequencing where appropriate



Avoid excessive bouncing.



Avoid elastic effects for editorial typography.



The movement should feel physical and controlled.



\---



\# 10 — PHOTOGRAPHY SYSTEM



Photography is one of the primary design materials.



Images should feel:



\- Architectural

\- High resolution

\- Cinematic

\- Natural

\- Warm

\- Detailed

\- Believable



Preferred imagery:



\- Finished interiors

\- Kitchens

\- Bathrooms

\- Extensions

\- Living spaces

\- Architectural details

\- Materials

\- Craftspeople

\- Construction details

\- Designers at work



Avoid generic corporate stock photography.



\---



\# 11 — IMAGE COMPOSITION



Images should NOT repeatedly appear as simple rectangular cards.



Avoid:



TEXT | IMAGE



as the default layout.



Preferred compositions:



\- Full-bleed

\- Edge-to-edge

\- Oversized

\- Cropped

\- Layered

\- Masked

\- Angled

\- Asymmetrical

\- Background imagery

\- Image overlapping multiple sections

\- Image passing behind typography



Photography may intentionally cross the boundaries of the normal grid.



\---



\# 12 — IMAGE OVERLAYS



When typography overlaps photography, use visual treatment to maintain readability.



Possible treatments:



\- Soft black gradient

\- Warm dark gradient

\- Localized blur

\- Vignette

\- Shadow

\- Reduced image exposure

\- Atmospheric haze



The overlay should be subtle.



Do NOT heavily blur the entire image simply to make text readable.



The image should remain beautiful.



\---



\# 13 — IMAGE DEPTH



Create depth using multiple visual layers.



Example:



BACKGROUND IMAGE

\+

COLOR / GRADIENT LAYER

\+

ARCHITECTURAL DETAIL

\+

TYPOGRAPHY

\+

FOREGROUND ELEMENT



Different layers may move at slightly different rates during scrolling.



This creates depth without requiring heavy 3D rendering.



\---



\# 14 — REACTIVE SCROLLING

\# NON-NEGOTIABLE



Scrolling must be reactive.



The page must respond continuously to the user's scroll position.



Do NOT rely primarily on:



"scroll into viewport → fade in → stop."



That is insufficient for this project.



Reactive scrolling means the current scroll position should continuously influence visual properties.



Possible properties include:



\- Position

\- Scale

\- Opacity

\- Rotation

\- Blur

\- Clip-path

\- Width

\- Height

\- Image crop

\- Typography position

\- Background position

\- Color

\- Mask size

\- Horizontal translation

\- Vertical translation



The relationship between scrolling and animation should feel continuous.



\---



\# 15 — SCROLL-LINKED ANIMATION



Where appropriate, animations should be tied directly to scroll progress.



Example:



At 0% scroll progress:



Image scale = 1.10



At 50%:



Image scale = 1.04



At 100%:



Image scale = 1.00



This should create a continuous transformation rather than a triggered animation.



Other examples:



Scroll progress

→ image moves horizontally



Scroll progress

→ headline moves upward



Scroll progress

→ image clipping changes



Scroll progress

→ background color transitions



Scroll progress

→ project navigation advances



Scroll progress

→ horizontal gallery progresses



\---



\# 16 — REACTIVE HERO



The hero should respond continuously to scrolling.



As the user begins scrolling:



\- Hero image slowly scales

\- Image position shifts

\- Typography moves independently

\- Overlay intensity changes

\- Hero content begins to leave the viewport

\- Next section begins entering



The transition should feel like the camera is moving through the hero rather than simply scrolling past it.



Avoid abrupt disappearance.



\---



\# 17 — PARALLAX



Use layered parallax.



Different elements may move at different speeds.



Example:



Background image

→ slow



Foreground architectural detail

→ medium



Typography

→ faster



Small metadata

→ subtle



The difference should be restrained.



Avoid extreme parallax that causes visual instability.



\---



\# 18 — STICKY STORYTELLING



Some sections should remain pinned while the content changes based on scroll.



Example:



PROCESS SECTION



Left side:



"How we work"



Right side:



01 Consultation

02 Design

03 Build

04 Handover



As the user scrolls:



The section remains pinned.



The active process step changes.



The image changes.



The description changes.



The progress indicator advances.



The user feels like they are moving through the process while remaining inside the same visual environment.



\---



\# 19 — HORIZONTAL SCROLL



Horizontal movement may be created through vertical scrolling.



Appropriate use cases:



\- Services

\- Projects

\- Image galleries

\- Transformation stories



Example:



User scrolls vertically.



The page pins the section.



The content moves horizontally.



Once the horizontal story is complete, normal vertical scrolling resumes.



This should feel intentional.



Do not use horizontal scrolling everywhere.



\---



\# 20 — BEFORE / AFTER INTERACTION



Before/after sections should not always be simple sliders.



Possible interaction:



Initial state:



BEFORE



As the user scrolls:



The image mask moves.



The new image gradually replaces the old image.



At the end:



AFTER



The transition should feel like the space is being transformed.



\---



\# 21 — IMAGE REVEAL SYSTEM



Images can be revealed through:



\- Circular masks

\- Vertical masks

\- Horizontal masks

\- Diagonal masks

\- Clip-path

\- Scale reveals

\- Cropped viewport movement



Use different reveal styles for different major sections.



Do not use the same image reveal everywhere.



\---



\# 22 — PROJECT TRANSITIONS



When moving between projects:



\- Current project image should transition naturally

\- Typography may remain anchored

\- Next project image may enter from another direction

\- Project number should update

\- Metadata should update

\- Navigation should respond to the change



Avoid basic carousel behavior whenever possible.



\---



\# 23 — NAVIGATION



The navigation should be minimal.



Possible structure:



LOGO



Work

Services

Process

About

Journal



CTA



Navigation behavior:



At page load:

→ transparent / integrated into hero



After scrolling:

→ subtle background or contrast change



When entering dark sections:

→ automatically adapt for readability



On mobile:

→ elegant fullscreen or overlay menu



Navigation transitions should be smooth.



\---



\# 24 — BUTTONS



Buttons should feel tactile.



Primary button:



\- Strong contrast

\- Minimal radius

\- Controlled hover

\- Subtle movement



Possible interaction:



Hover:



Arrow shifts slightly.



Button background changes.



Text moves subtly.



Cursor interaction may be added where appropriate.



Do not create exaggerated magnetic effects.



\---



\# 25 — CURSOR INTERACTION



Desktop may use a custom cursor system.



Possible states:



Default

→ minimal cursor



Image hover

→ "View"



Project hover

→ project title / arrow



Interactive element

→ subtle enlargement



The cursor should remain understated.



Do not make it a gimmick.



Disable or simplify on touch devices.



\---



\# 26 — SECTION TRANSITIONS



Sections should feel connected.



Possible transitions:



\- Image continues into next section

\- Background color morphs

\- Typography crosses section boundary

\- Image scales into a new composition

\- Dark section gradually emerges

\- Large project image becomes the next hero



Avoid:



Section ends

→ blank gap

→ next section starts.



The entire page should feel like one continuous composition.



\---



\# 27 — SPACING



Use generous whitespace.



Spacing should create rhythm.



Not every section needs the same vertical padding.



Some sections should breathe.



Others should feel dense and immersive.



Use spacing to control pacing.



\---



\# 28 — GRID SYSTEM



Use a consistent underlying grid.



The grid should provide structure without becoming visually obvious everywhere.



Allow controlled grid-breaking compositions.



Examples:



\- Oversized typography

\- Images extending beyond grid

\- Offset columns

\- Diagonal boundaries

\- Asymmetric project layouts



The grid is a foundation, not a cage.



\---



\# 29 — BORDERS AND DIVIDERS



Use borders sparingly.



Preferred:



\- Fine lines

\- Low contrast

\- Warm grey

\- Subtle opacity



Borders should help organize information.



They should not make the website look like a dashboard.



\---



\# 30 — TEXTURE



Subtle texture may be used to avoid an overly sterile digital appearance.



Possible:



\- Fine grain

\- Paper-like texture

\- Architectural material texture

\- Very subtle noise



Keep texture extremely restrained.



The user should feel it rather than consciously notice it.



\---

Each major section should have its own visual composition.



HOME

→ cinematic



SERVICES

→ editorial / asymmetric



PROCESS

→ structured / immersive



PROJECT

→ architectural / photographic



TRANSFORMATION

→ interactive



ARCHIVE

ABOUT

→ typography-led



JOURNAL

CONTACT

Do not turn all sections into variations of the same component.



\---



\# 32 — RESPONSIVE DESIGN



The visual identity must survive across:



\- Desktop

\- Laptop

\- Tablet

\- Mobile



Desktop may contain:



\- Complex parallax

\- Horizontal scroll

\- Cursor interactions

\- Larger typography

\- Layered imagery



Mobile should retain the storytelling but simplify expensive interactions where necessary.



Do not create horizontal overflow accidentally.



Do not force desktop compositions onto mobile.



\---



\# 33 — MOTION PERFORMANCE



All high-end animation must remain performant.



Prefer:



\- transform

\- opacity

\- scale

\- translate

\- clip-path where appropriate



Avoid unnecessarily animating:



\- width

\- height

\- top

\- left

\- margin

\- padding



unless there is a strong reason.



Use GPU-friendly animation wherever possible.



Large images must be optimized.



Animations should not cause visible frame drops on normal hardware.



\---



\# 34 — ACCESSIBILITY



Motion should respect reduced-motion preferences.



If the user has reduced motion enabled:



\- Disable intense parallax

\- Reduce transitions

\- Remove large scroll-linked transformations

\- Preserve content hierarchy

\- Maintain usability



Accessibility should not destroy the visual identity.



It should provide a calmer version of it.



\---



\# 35 — RESPONSIVE MOTION RULE



The animation system should not simply shrink desktop animations.



It should adapt.



Desktop:



Complex scroll choreography.



Tablet:



Reduced choreography.



Mobile:



Focused transitions with fewer simultaneous moving elements.



The storytelling should remain.



The complexity can change.



\---



\# 36 — VISUAL HIERARCHY



At any moment the user should understand:



1\. Where they are

2\. What they are looking at

3\. What matters

4\. Where they can go next



Visual experimentation must never destroy navigation or comprehension.



\---



\# 37 — DESIGN QUALITY CHECK



Before accepting any section, ask:



Does this look like a template?



If yes:

→ redesign.



Does the image feel like a card placed beside content?



If yes:

→ redesign.



Could this layout appear unchanged on ten contractor websites?



If yes:

→ redesign.



Is the animation merely a fade-in?



If yes:

→ consider a more meaningful interaction.



Does the animation exist only to show technical skill?



If yes:

→ remove it.



Does the interaction improve storytelling?



If yes:

→ keep it.



Does the section look impressive as a screenshot AND feel impressive while scrolling?



Both are required.

The final website should feel like:



A premium architectural editorial

\+

A creative studio portfolio

\+

A cinematic brand film

\+

A highly polished commercial website



It should NOT feel like:



A template

\+

A contractor directory

\+

A SaaS landing page

\+

A collection of animated cards.



\---



\# 39 — FINAL RULE



The design system should always prioritize:



DESIGN

→ STORY

→ INTERACTION

→ USABILITY

→ PERFORMANCE



Not:



ANIMATION

→ ANIMATION

→ ANIMATION.



Motion exists to make the design feel alive.



The website should feel reactive, intentional and physical.



The user should feel that their scrolling is actively controlling the experience.
